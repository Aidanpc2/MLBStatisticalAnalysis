##The Null hypothesis is that there is no difference in luck between years meaning that this is just who these players are. The alternative hypothesis is that the players had luckier results in 2025 meaning that 2024 was more of a fluke and less of a certainty of who this player is.

#The statistical test I am running is a paired t-test because we are comparing the means of the same set of players in one specific statistic between 2 years.

#We are running this test at a 0.05 significance level

t.test(UnluckyHitting2$difference_2025, UnluckyHitting2$difference_2024, paired = TRUE, alternative = "greater", conf.level = 0.95) #run a paired t-test comparing the 2025 difference and 2024 difference to see if the mean if the 5 unluckiest hitters in 2024 were as unluckier, unluckier, or luckier (alternative hypothesis) in 2025

##Research Question 2: A player’s prime is typically between the ages of 26 and 29. The league average OPS (On Base Percentage + Slugging Percentage) is 0.719. Let’s say a significantly above average OPS is 0.750, is the average OPS of players in their primes significantly above average?

prime2026 <- hitting %>% #create new data frame using the hitting data set
  filter(year == 2026)%>% #use only the data from 2026
  filter(age >= 26 & age <= 29)%>% #only keep players aged 26 through 29
  select(name,year,age,on_base_plus_slg) #select relevant columns

#The null hypothesis is that players in their primes are not “significantly” greater than league average in terms of OPS (0.750) and the alternative hypothesis is that their average is greater than 0.750.

#The test I plan on using is a one sample t-test because we are comparing one statistic to a set number (0.750)

##This test is being ran at a 0.95 significance level

t.test(prime2026$on_base_plus_slg, mu = 0.750, alternative = "greater",conf.level = 0.95) #Run a one sample t-test to see if the mean OPS of players in their prime is "significantly" higher than the league average OPS

##  One Sample t-test
## 
## data:  prime2026$on_base_plus_slg

##Because the p-value is greater than 0.05 (0.25) we fail to reject the null hypothesis therefore declaring that the mean OPS of players in their primes is NOT significantly greater than the league average OPS
